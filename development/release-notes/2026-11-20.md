# Release Notes: 20 November 2026

## 3. Assessment by Evaluators of Many Kinds

heuresis has a new module, `assessment` ([decision 0010](../decisions/0010-assessment.md)), for searches whose candidates are judged by closed forms, simulations, agent models, surrogates, language models and people at once.

- **Every number says what kind of evidence it is.** Each metric carries its uncertainty and its standing: verified, computed, estimated or proposed. A metric can never claim more than its evaluator: a model that says its opinion is verified is recorded as proposing.
- **Strong evidence decides first.** Candidates are ordered by tiers: failures last, then binding constraints, then the objectives strong enough to select; estimates and proposals only break ties and describe. An evolution judged by a computed loss and a model that wants the opposite finds the computed optimum.
- **Noise where it costs.** Replicates by a power calculation; races that select the best with a stated probability, the empirical Bernstein race spending a tenth of Hoeffding's samples where the variance is small; common random numbers kept only where they help; cascades from cheap to costly evaluators that select as an exhaustive evaluation would, at 6% of its cost on the checks' instances.
- **And more:** a cache of every assessment, kept in order for sealing; whether a surrogate can be trusted, with the reason when it cannot; Bradley–Terry for pairwise judgements, with the advantage of being shown first.
- **Checks:** 30.

## 2. Orders of Grants

heuresis has a new module, `sequential` ([decision 0009](../decisions/0009-orders-of-grants.md)), for allocations made one grant at a time, where giving A first and then B can differ from B first and then A.

- **Which orders matter.** A system of grants acting on a state can declare what each grant reads and writes, and have it tested: both orders are tried at states the system can reach, and a pair that fails to commute is reported with how that state was reached. Two claimants of the last unit of a stock are found not to commute, though neither order can satisfy both. A declaration that the tests refute is reported as a contradiction.
- **Each order evaluated once.** Orders that differ only by exchanging grants that commute are one trace, and the evolutionary loop now evaluates each trace once: 720 orders of six grants on three stocks cost 8 evaluations.
- **Decoders that say what they reach.** Reading an order as an allocation by skipping, deferring or stopping at a grant that cannot be made, or step by step in time, each with the allocations it can and cannot reach, held to brute force on a hundred random systems.
- **Operators by what they keep.** Crossovers that keep shared precedences, shared adjacencies or shared positions, and a probe that says which of the three an objective depends on. On a stock that runs out, operators that keep precedence do best; on setups between consecutive items, edge recombination does, as Starkweather and his colleagues found in 1991.
- **And more:** picking sequences and other orders in which a letter recurs, as a space; Mallows's laws on orders under three metrics, drawn exactly and fitted; and a bracket on how much a policy that watches outcomes could gain over a fixed order, held on stochastic knapsacks to the bound of 4 that Dean, Goemans and Vondrák proved.
- **Checks:** 48.

## 1. Typed Spaces

heuresis has a new module, `spaces` ([decision 0008](../decisions/0008-typed-spaces.md)), in which a search over values of many kinds at once can be stated and checked.

- **A space for each kind of value.** Real and integer boxes, multisets, categories, ordered levels, shares of a whole in Aitchison's geometry, permutations under Cayley's, Hamming's or Kendall's metric, subsets of bounded size, identical units shared among parts with caps, sequences of any space's values, the sphere and the rotations of space. Each says how to draw a value, how far a mutation moves, how to measure distance, and what a typical distance is, in closed form where one exists.
- **Spaces built from spaces.** A product of named parts, weighted so that no part dominates for being wide; a choice among branches; a subspace cut out by a rule, with a repair or with its rejections counted. A product's part-wise crossover stays on the segment between its parents, as Moraglio's theorem says.
- **Laws for any space.** One function holds any space, a person's as well as the built-in ones, to its laws: valid values, a metric, mutations within their ball, children on the segment where that is claimed, repairs that settle, charts that invert, embeddings that decode, the unit it declares. A law that fails is reported with its worst case, so that a search knows what its operators do.
- **What a representation does.** Measures of a representation through any decoder show, for example, that random keys leave an order unchanged in nine small mutations of ten, where a direct permutation never does.
- **Into the evolutionary loop.** One adapter gives any space's operators to `evolution`; an evolution over seven spaces at once finds a known optimum.
- **Checks:** 83, among them every pair of ten finite spaces against their closed forms, 3,000 children of random products, and four faults planted in a broken space, each found.
