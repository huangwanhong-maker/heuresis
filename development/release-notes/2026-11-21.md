# Release Notes: 21 November 2026

## Surrogates Whose Intervals Can Be Trusted, and Policies Set Against Sequences

A surrogate of a costly evaluator can now give intervals that cover the truth as often as they promise ([decision 0011](../decisions/0011-calibrated-surrogates-and-policies-against-sequences.md)): split conformal intervals around a Gaussian process, calibrated on runs it was not fitted on, at 90% covering 90.2% of new runs over two hundred trials. A screen keeps every candidate whose interval could hold the best and asks the true evaluator only about those, so the best is not lost while the intervals cover, and it says how often they did.

An allocation can now be made by a policy as well as by a sequence: a rule that scores each allowed grant from the state and grants the best, learned once for a family of instances, linear or as a program. On knapsacks whose price rises with every grant, a policy learned once reached 95% of each new instance's best order, where a sequence searched for each instance needed thirty evaluations of its own to do better.
