# Release Notes: 5 November 2026

## 1. heuresis Founded: Search over Spaces Too Large to Enumerate

heuresis is the library that searches for solutions: designs, plans, controllers, programs and structures, in spaces too large to enumerate and without a gradient to follow ([decision 0002](../decisions/0002-scope-boundaries-and-dependencies.md)). Evolutionary computation is its first and largest part, with the other global, stochastic and black-box searches beside it.

- **An algorithm never calls its objective** ([decision 0003](../decisions/0003-the-search-process-and-its-seams.md)). It asks for evaluations and is told them, so the same algorithm runs on one thread, a pool of threads, remote workers, a device or with a person in the loop.
- **Placement does not change a run.** Every draw is keyed by what it is for. A generational run is the same, ticket for ticket, whatever the order in which its evaluations return, whether serial, on a pool, cut into parts, or on simulated workers.
- **Simulated workers** measure, in simulated wall time, what synchronising a generation costs on heterogeneous machines, before a search is spread over real ones.
- **Five seams** take what is not yet known:
  - executors;
  - representations and their kernels;
  - search distributions and their samplers, where a quantum device or an annealer is a sampler of a law whose exact emulation on the CPU is its reference;
  - migration between islands;
  - proposals from outside, such as a person or a language model, entering with their origin.
- **Eighteen modules** ([decision 0004](../decisions/0004-the-first-modules.md)), 492 checks, all passing:
  - the search process, representations and their kernels, selection, benchmarks with known optima and fronts;
  - evolutionary algorithms (the (1+(λ,λ)) GA among them), local search;
  - CMA-ES with its restarts, differential evolution and SHADE, particle swarms and ant colonies;
  - UMDA, PBIL, the compact GA, the cross-entropy method, Plackett–Luce;
  - quantum-inspired search and Grover adaptive search;
  - simulated annealing, tempering and population annealing;
  - NSGA-II, NSGA-III, SMS-EMOA and MOEA/D with exact hypervolumes;
  - novelty search and MAP-Elites;
  - programs typed by physical dimensions, grammar-guided programs and grammatical evolution;
  - island and cellular models, Bayesian optimisation, landscapes, and the analysis of runs.
- **Held to the theory**:
  - runtimes against the exact expectations of their Markov chains, and invariance under rotations and monotone maps to rounding;
  - Boltzmann laws, free energies, takeover times and hypervolumes against exact values;
  - Grover's law against a simulation of its amplitudes;
  - CMA-ES's update against pycma's to 5 × 10⁻¹⁶.
- **A theory note** ([docs/theory.md](../../docs/theory.md)) states what the checks rest on:
  - No Free Lunch, and invariance as a group action;
  - the information-geometric flow, of which PBIL and CMA-ES's rank-μ update are instances;
  - why quantum-inspired algorithms are product distributions;
  - Grover's law, and programs as typed terms.
